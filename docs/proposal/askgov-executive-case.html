02
The proposition

One sentence

A Cambodian citizen asks, in Khmer, what documents to bring, what it costs, how long it takes and which office to go to — and receives an answer a ministry has approved, with the instrument it came from printed underneath.

Access

Free. No registration. Any hour. Khmer and English.

Where

A mini-app inside the DG Super App, and a standalone web application on a government domain.

The constraint

It answers only from approved government sources. Where it has none, it declines and hands the citizen to an officer.

Say this

“Everything else in this paper follows from the third box. It is the reason this is a government service and not a chatbot.”

03
Mandate

This is not a new idea. It is existing policy, delivered.

  • Cambodia Digital Government Policy 2022–2035 — to improve quality of life and public confidence through better public service.
  • Cambodia Digital Economy and Society Policy Framework 2021–2035 — building digital citizens and digital government.
  • Pentagonal Strategy — Phase 1 — human capital and the digital economy.

Every one of these commits the government to better public service through digital means. None of them is served by a citizen who cannot find out what documents a birth certificate requires.

Say this

“We are not asking the Committee to adopt a new direction. We are asking it to instruct on one it has already adopted.”

04
The problem, at citizen scale

Three ordinary days that go wrong

Mala — new mother

Needs a birth certificate for school enrolment. Does not know a statutory registration deadline exists, that late registration is a different procedure, or which office holds it. She learns the penalty at the counter.

Ratana — first passport

Does not know where passports are actually issued, the opening hours, the fee or the processing time. Takes an unpaid day off and arrives at the wrong office.

Sopheak — small trader

First international shipment. Does not know the prohibited items, the customs declaration or the tariff. Goes to the counter to ask, then goes back a second time with the right packaging.

In every case the correct answer already existed in an official document. In every case it did not reach the person who needed it.

Who pays  A second trip is transport, lost wages and, from a province, a lost day. The cost of bad information falls on the citizen, and heaviest on the citizens least able to absorb it.

06
Why it persists

Inside government, this failure is invisible — and that is what sustains it

  • Because the questions are asked to neighbours rather than to the state, no ministry has any record of what citizens are confused about.
  • There is no signal when a procedural change has failed to reach the public.
  • There is no evidence base for deciding which service page to fix first.
  • Officers answer the same questions individually, thousands of times, with no accumulation.
  • We are digitising services faster than we are digitising the explanation of them. Every new online service adds procedural questions to a channel that does not exist.
The market that fills the gap  An informal intermediary market charges citizens for information the state publishes for free, and its commercial interest is that the procedure stays opaque. Without intervention that market grows with every release.
Say this

“The demand data alone would be worth building. Today no one in this government can answer the question ‘what are citizens failing to find out?’ — because nobody is asking us.”

07 · The rule that governs the entire design
A confident wrong answer about a fee, a deadline or an office is worse for a citizen than no answer at all.

Because the citizen acts on it. They travel, they pay, they miss a statutory deadline, and they lose money or a legal right.

So AskGov answers only from content a designated ministry steward has approved. Where it holds no approved source, it declines to answer and routes the citizen to a human officer rather than produce a plausible guess. It never guesses.

This is a governance decision approved at leadership level, not an engineering preference.  BR-001 · BR-002 · G2

08
How an answer is made

Three tiers, five gates

Tier 1 — Curated

A steward-approved answer served verbatim. No model involved at all.
Target at maturity: 50–70%

Tier 2 — Sourced

The model composes an answer only from retrieved approved text, and cites what it used.
Target at maturity: 25–45%

Tier 3 — Refusal

A plain explanation, plus a human officer or a named responsible office. Never a dead end.
Target at maturity: 5–10%

The five gates every answer must pass

1

Refusal policy — applied before any retrieval or model call. Emergencies, personal cases, legal advice, political questions and complaints never reach the engine. FR-13

2

Confidence floor — if retrieval is weak, the system does not generate at all. A fluent answer written over irrelevant sources is the precise failure this project exists to prevent. FR-19

3

Topicality — a highly ranked source that does not address the subject is withheld, not used. This is what stops an adjacent ministry's rules being served as yours. FR-20

4

Verification — every figure, date, quantity and citation in the drafted answer is checked against the cited source. Anything unsupported means the answer is suppressed entirely — never partially delivered, never automatically corrected. FR-22 · BR-004

5

Moderation — an answer that has drifted into advising, guaranteeing an outcome or refused territory is suppressed before delivery. FR-23

Say this

“Gate 4 is the one that matters to you. A fabricated fee cannot reach a citizen even when the model produces one.”

10
Deliberate limits

What AskGov will never do — and why that protects the Committee

Refused by approved policy

Emergencies (terminates with verified numbers) · personal case lookup · legal advice or opinion on merits · prediction of an official decision · land disputes · individual tax computation · political commentary · complaint intake · anything that is not a government service.

Outside the service entirely

It does not transact — no applications, no payments, no appointments. It does not become the legal publication channel; the instrument remains authoritative. It never cites a news outlet, a private firm or a broker site.

Say this

“Every line on the left is a headline we have decided in advance not to generate. And the refusal policy is approved as a governance instrument at leadership level — so no engineer owns that decision, and no engineer can change it.”

11
Delivery

Two channels, one engine

DG Super App mini-app

The promoted route. Mobile only, and it presupposes a super app account. It brings a high-demand information service into the super app as a first-use entry point and a repeat-use driver. IS-24 · G12

Standalone web application

On a registered government domain, responsive from a low-cost handset to a desktop. The only route on a laptop, and the only fully anonymous route. IS-25 · BR-030

Both are served by one answer engine, one approved corpus and one set of governance controls. The channel changes packaging and presentation — never what AskGov answers, refuses, cites or escalates. Where a citizen is signed in to the super app, AskGov does not receive, request, store or infer that identity in Phase 1. FR-72 · FR-80 · FR-82

Schedule note  The mini-app is gated by the DG Super App release cycle, a dependency outside this project's control. The two channels are deliberately independent so a freeze on one cannot block the other. NFR-32 · OD-10 · D-06

Three consequences for the design

  • Mobile-first is the specification, not a preference. A low-cost Android handset on mobile data is the target device, in both the mini-app runtime and the browser. NFR-07 · NFR-31
  • Ministries announce on Facebook and Telegram before, or instead of, their websites.   • Khmer costs more output tokens than English. Latency budgets and running cost must be measured in Khmer, not extrapolated from English. NFR-06
Measure, don't assume  The Khmer token multiplier and the per-answer cost should be measured on the existing R&D prototype before any figure is quoted to procurement.

14
An uncomfortable number, stated now rather than later

Launch will not look like maturity

The tier mix on slide 8 — refusal at 5–10% — describes a mature corpus, after many institutions have onboarded. At launch, with Group 1 content only, most questions a citizen asks will fall outside the corpus, and the system will do exactly what it was designed to do: refuse and offer an officer.

At launch

Expect Tier 3 refusal in the region of 30–50% of in-scope questions. This is correct behaviour, not a defect.

At maturity

5–10%, converging as institutions onboard and curated answers accumulate.

Estimate, not measurement  The 30–50% range is derived from the size of the launch corpus relative to expected question breadth. It must be validated against the golden set on the prototype before it is quoted anywhere it will be held to.
Say this

“If the Committee is told to expect 5–10% refusal at launch, this project will be judged a failure in month one for behaving exactly as designed. I am asking for two targets — a launch target and a maturity target — reported against separately.”

Where content comes from

Three acquisition modes — and where the value actually is

ModeApplies toMethodCost
A · MonitorAlready published on an official website or designated channelAutomated monitoring, change detection, steward reviewLow
B · TransferHeld by the institution but unpublished — circulars, service standards, counter notices, formsStructured handover under an MoU; ingestion and steward approvalMedium
C · Co-createExists only as officer practice and has never been written downJoint working sessions with front-line officers, field capture, drafting, legal review, steward approvalHigh

Mode C is the distinguishing capability of this programme. For those procedures AskGov is not competing with an existing information source — it is creating the only one.

It is also slower and more expensive per procedure than A and B, and it is where the political sensitivity lives. That trade-off is the substance of the direction choice on slide 20.

19
Operating in our landscape

Seven political considerations that shape how this must be run

1

The mandate converts every conversation. Without a Royal Government or Council of Ministers-level instruction, or an inter-ministerial circular, every institutional MoU is a request for a favour. With one, it is an implementation discussion. This is the single highest-leverage action available to the sponsor, and nothing else in the paper substitutes for it. D-01

2

Approach the policy owner first, always. For any domain: secure the ministry's mandate, then engage the general department, then external partners under that mandate. Approaching front-line officers, the Bar Association or a development partner before the institution has agreed will be read as bypassing the institution, and it will cost the relationship permanently.

3

Frame the intermediary problem without accusation. The defensible sentence is “citizens pay for information the state publishes for free.” It is the same fact, it is verifiable, and it names no one.

4

Political neutrality is existential, not cosmetic. A service carrying the Committee's name that comments on politics, named officials or contested land disputes becomes a headline. That is precisely why refusal is approved as a governance instrument at leadership level and is not a configuration setting an engineer can change. OD-07 · BR-001

5

Sovereignty is an asset here, not only a constraint. Sarika at Phase 1 and a credible, dated domestic-inference path let the sponsor describe this as Cambodian capability rather than a foreign system in national colours. The commitment is worth more than the technology.

6

Sub-national reality will contradict national content. A national answer that is wrong at a district counter damages the institution that supplied it. The rule requiring the answer to name the applicable administration, or state that local variation exists, must be built in before Group 4 — not retrofitted after the first complaint. BR-028

7

Give institutions the credit, and give the content back. Every answer names the ministry. Approved content is exported for the institution to publish on its own channels, so AskGov is never the sole location of public procedural information. That single clause turns a perceived threat into a service, and it is why institutions sign. BR-027 · FR-52

21
Recommendation

Launch on A. Start C's engagement in parallel. Defer B.

  • A is the only direction that produces a defensible public launch inside a year, and it is where the quality metrics are measured against real citizens for the first time. We should not scale a pipeline that has never run.
  • But Mode C takes 12–18 months, and its early stages cost the delivery team nothing. Stages 2 to 6 are introductions, demonstrations, scoping and legal review — meetings and paperwork. They do not compete with the engineers. If we begin the Ministry of Justice engagement only after Group 1 launches, we lose a year for no saving at all.
  • B is not a direction; it is a consequence. Breadth arrives by adding institutions to a proven pipeline, not by signing ten memoranda before the pipeline exists.

A is the launch. C is the reason the project matters. B is what year two looks like if A works.

Say this

“The only irreversible decision in front of the Committee today is the one about time. Signing the mandate now and starting the justice introduction this quarter costs nothing and saves a year. Everything else can be adjusted later.”

26 · Closing
The correct answer already exists. Our job is not to invent it.

It is in a Prakas, a sub-decree, a service standard, or in the head of the officer standing at the counter. AskGov's job is to carry it — unchanged, with its source attached — to the citizen who needs it, and to say so plainly when it cannot.

The decision in front of the Committee is not whether the technology works. It is whether the government will instruct its institutions to release what they already know, and to whom that instruction is addressed.
